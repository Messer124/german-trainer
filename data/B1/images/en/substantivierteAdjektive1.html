Adjectives used as nouns are quite common in German. This phenomenon is called nominalization of adjectives.

A nominalized adjective is capitalized and used with articles or article-like words as a noun, but it is declined for gender, number and case like an adjective; that means it takes the usual adjective endings.

Example:

alt (old)
→
der Alte, ein Alter (old man)
→
die Alte, eine Alte (old woman)
→
das Alte, etwas Altes (something old / the old thing)

Nouns can also be formed from adjectives in the comparative and superlative forms: etwas Schöneres (something more beautiful), die Beste (the best one), and also from participles — der Angestellte (the employee).

Nominalized adjectives are used:

  1. to refer to people (masculine and feminine gender, singular and plural) — mein Bekannter (my acquaintance), die Arbeitslosen (the unemployed), der Deutsche (the German man).
  2. to express abstract ideas (neuter gender, singular) — etwas Bekanntes (something familiar), das Grüne (something green; do not confuse it with das Grün — the color green).
